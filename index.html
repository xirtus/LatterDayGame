The work of gathering begins with a question.

The design decision

A life of revelation that becomes a work of gathering

Build an immersive, positive Latter-day Saint historical adventure in which the player is Joseph Smith: first a questioning boy, then a translator, husband, preacher, organizer, prisoner, and prophet. Let every major encounter be a lived place with people, tasks, conversations, and room to look. The unifying action is gathering: bringing scripture to readers, families to safety, the bereaved to hope, and a people toward Zion.

The working title is Joseph Smith and the Gathering of Zion. The primary campaign treats Joseph's reported revelations and prophetic calling as authentic within its world. It does not make religious belief a question the player must disprove. Earthly life has weather, work, mistakes, friendship, humor, tenderness, disagreement, and real suffering. Spiritual authenticity need not turn every administrative decision into an infallible success or every critic into a villain.

The ending is playable hope. Carthage is followed by an imagined spirit-world chapter and a substantial celestial stewardship level. The last action is to welcome someone and help a living world flourish. The optional second campaign extends that vocation into a seven-era mission by Saints from a future Zion.

The chapter durations are initial content assumptions, not playtest results. They total approximately 14.1 hours on the directed path, before optional exploration and longer conversations. A realistic first release can be chapter-based rather than a continuous simulation of every year and mile. Nothing in this report requires an open-world recreation of the entire United States.

The super narrative

  1. Ask. A boy seeks guidance among competing voices; a personal relationship with God becomes the root of his calling.
  2. Receive and learn. The entrusted record, family losses, repentance, translation, and restored authority make preparation a serious responsibility.
  3. Gather. A church, a school, a temple, and communities give the calling a social form. Zion becomes a home people must build together.
  4. Endure and console. Failure, expulsion, prison, and bereavement test whether that home can be rooted in persuasion and care. King Follett gives the cosmic vision a human purpose.
  5. Continue. Martyrdom does not end the work. The final garden and the optional mission make eternal progression a growing capacity to love and create.

Religious perspective and historical precision

Make the distinctions once and keep the experience sincere

The opening title card can say: “A devotional historical drama. Reported revelations are portrayed from a Latter-day Saint perspective. Private dialogue is dramatized. The celestial and time-travel chapters are imaginative interpretations.” The journal then distinguishes a documented action, a participant's account, an original dramatic conversation, and speculative theology. These distinctions need not interrupt prayer, acting, or exploration with repeated disclaimers.

The setting can contain lodges, sermons, print disputes, biblical arguments, and friendship across beliefs. Zion's promise of refuge becomes stronger when the game respects the humanity of those outside it. Adaptation of the Celandia style bible

Nineteenth-century light and a procedural illustrated world

The visual target is a mystical American nineteenth century rendered through Celandia's warm ink, sculpted forms, colored shadows, and restrained radiance. Let the steampunk resemblance come from brass instruments, hand-operated presses, mill wheels, riverboats, joinery, coats, and early industrial craft that belong to the period. There are no airships, clockwork automatons, electric streetlights in 1820, or arbitrary fantasy engines in the historical campaign.

Warm inkCream lightCanopy oliveTimberLiving waterLavender shade

Retain the single style module, four-band toon materials, vertex-color variation, depth-and-normal outlines, and a small shared palette. Start from Celandia's measured hemisphere and sun rig: 1.95 and 1.45, warm sun at 55 degrees, no tone mapping, exposure 1, outline ink #2a1a14, approximately 1.6 pixels near and 0.8 far at the reference resolution, bloom strength 0.35. These are starting values for a prototype, not a claim that every night interior must share daylight illumination. Author named night, prison, room, and vision presets and approve them with their own references.

Replace the fantasy species with regional hardwoods, farm crops, prairie grasses, wetland plants, orchard trees, and appropriate animals. Replace crooked fairy cottages with date-appropriate timber and brick kits. Use ordinary human proportions and recognizably different faces. Keep cosmic auroras and impossible skies for revelations and the explicitly speculative chapters. Earthly skies should let celestial intervention feel distinct.

What code-only should mean

Generate terrain, buildings, furniture, props, plants, water, particles, characters, and much environmental sound from code and deterministic parameters. A library of authored procedures is still authored art. The game will contain assets in the ordinary sense: geometry, fonts, voices, captions, music, manifests, and source documents. “No imported model or texture packs” is a useful direction; “literally no assets” would contradict voiced performances and concept art.

For characters, retain two compatible production routes: a human anatomy and wardrobe kit adapted from Celandia's procedural builders, and carefully reviewed imports of the user's Meshy character prototypes. The latter are now explicit candidates for geometry and rig reuse; the environment can remain procedurally generated. Create Joseph at four ages, Emma through several life stages, distinctive heads, editable hair shapes, coats, waistcoats, dresses, shawls, trousers, boots, and cloth panels. Keep skeleton proportions and face identity separate from costume. Use contact poses, foot placement, eye targets, blinking, breath, hands, and several visemes. Close-up faces, hands on paper, and a convincing embrace are major production work, not automatic consequences of having a generated mesh. The character design and Meshy audit defines the new candidates and modeling order.

Each essential encounter needs an establishing composition, a player-eye view, a two-shot, a reaction, a meaningful prop insert, and an inspection orbit. Concepts are initial design proposals; precise room plans, costume references, anatomical turnarounds, lighting keys, and in-engine acting remain the approval path. The forty panels cover all core chapters plus the expansion's eras and portal finale. Their captions identify atmosphere and narrative, not documentary photographs or approved final rendering.

A concept acceptance sheet for every chapter

Before building a chapter, give it one agreed plan with a dated map, a floor plan, entrances, named speakers, prop references, Joseph's age, one emotional objective, essential actions, sources, adapted dialogue, camera anchors, audio cues, and a persistence checkpoint. Before locking it, verify from six directions that doors, floors, faces, hands, unseen walls, and sightlines all work. A beautiful illustration alone does not establish a traversable scene.

Use three approved in-engine reference scenes as the new visual contract: the Sacred Grove, the Harmony translation room, and the Nauvoo street. Celandia's old image metrics contain known limitations: its current OpenCV Lab conversion, nondeterministic clustering, and automatic resizing need correction before numerical gates are trusted. Candidate matched-view thresholds such as SSIM 0.90 and mean/p95 color difference 3/6 are inherited targets, not measurements this report has passed. A prison scene must be compared against its own approved prison reference, not forced to match an olive grove histogram.

How to turn these into our cel-shaded people

  1. Keep the originals. Work on a derived version for each candidate. Match anatomy, costume year, height and head identity to the chosen sheet before changing the shader.
  2. Choose a sensible mesh. Start around ten-to-twenty thousand triangles for an ordinary principal-character prototype, with a higher-detail close-up version where acting requires it. These are starting budgets to test, not claims that a triangle count alone makes a face good. Remesh before final rigging, following Meshy's documented post-processing order.
  3. Separate color from illumination. Many candidates put skin, hair and every clothing piece into one material and a single atlas. Keep UVs if useful, but create flat albedo regions or masks for skin, hair, coat, shirt and shoes. Remove baked lighting, pore detail and cloth grain. Simply applying a toon material to a shaded photograph can preserve muddy double shadows.
  4. Apply the shared rendering style. Use a nearest-filtered three- or four-band ramp, no photographic normal/roughness detail, a restrained warm fill and sun, and a skinning-compatible warm ink outline. Three's toon material supplies the banded lighting; final Celandia-style production can use the corresponding node-material pipeline. Smooth modeled face volumes can still receive crisp cel shadows.
  5. Disable inappropriate emission. Several rigged exports use their texture as an emissive map with full emissive factor. Carrying this over can make an earthly person ignore the scene's lighting. Reserve authored emission for the places that need it; sacred faces should also retain form.
  6. Repair costume deformation. Coat tails and dresses must not be fused to thighs or dragged around by unsuitable weights. Rework skirt/coat sections, provide suitable bones or constrained secondary motion, and test sitting, kneeling, stairs and turns. Emma's dress is a particularly useful early stress test.
  7. Prepare close-up acting. Add face controls and a shared hand strategy. The imported twenty-two-joint body should not be assumed to have Mixamo's full finger rig simply because its bones have Mixamo-like names. Meshy's animation documentation likewise distinguishes its preset motions from detailed cinematic facial work.
  8. Reduce texture cost deliberately. Several candidates retain 4096-square images even after geometry reduction. A single RGBA8 4K texture occupies about64 MiB uncompressed, or about85 MiB with its mip chain, before format-specific compression. Choose 1K/2K atlases, small masks or vertex colors where appropriate; keep detail where a close-up benefits.
  9. Approve in context. Review the same head and costume in a bright grove, a warm room, Liberty's low light, and the sermon crowd. Test original, textured-toon and clay views, but do not call the first shader swap a finished stylization.

The live material study illustrates that distinction: original material on the left, the same geometry with toon bands on the right, and a clay toggle to reveal the underlying shape. It intentionally retains the source color atlas for the textured test. This is a way to evaluate and preserve work, not an assertion that the baked atlas already satisfies the final art contract.

The King Follett scene as the acting benchmark

Target 25 to 35 minutes for the complete encounter, with an 8 to 12 minute adapted central address, optional longer contextual readings, and time before and after with mourners. The working lines below are a tone and blocking draft, not the entire final address. The source corpus has multiple partial reports, so production must maintain a passage-to-source table and never call the edited performance a complete verbatim transcript.

  1. Walk to the conference through working Nauvoo, greet the Follett family, and hear Louisa's particular worry. The player is present before becoming the speaker.
  2. Step to the stand, place notes or hands, pause, ask for God's guidance, and face the people. No timer punishes the pause.
  3. Choose a reviewed opening emphasis: shared grief, prayer for understanding, or the hope of resurrection. Each leads into the same doctrinal spine.
  4. Deliver linked sections about God, continuing personhood, learning, and exaltation. The player selects a listener, a pause, and an approved next passage rather than inventing doctrine.
  5. Let a brief star-field or eternal-growth visualization remain tied to the living stand and recognizable mourners. Free camera can view Joseph, the crowd, the scribes, or the symbolic space.
  6. Return fully to the family. End with a conversation and an ordinary act of care. The player should remember a grieving person before remembering the visual effect.

Local performance pipeline

Prepare MOSSlanding voices before final recording

The inspected MOSSlanding README describes a local voice library, short-reference voice cloning, direct/clone/continuation modes, and MOSS model choices. The macOS backend exposes /api/status, /api/voices, /api/voices/upload, and /api/synthesize. It returns WAV and saves a generated copy. Its actual sample rate comes from the loaded model. The older Linux handoff is a separate environment and should not be treated as a current macOS validation report.

A saved reference voice is not a trained per-character model. The inspected backend conditions generation on reference audio; it does not expose a character fine-tuning job. Prepare named character reference presets first. If a trained checkpoint for every principal role is desired later, that is a separate upstream-model training investigation. The report does not claim to have trained, recorded, or auditioned these voices.

  1. Cast the principal relationships first. Audition Joseph youth, Joseph adult, Emma, Oliver, Hyrum, Christ, Moroni, Louisa, and Orson against paired lines. Lock identities before generating hundreds of takes. Use the same adult performer for young/mature Joseph if possible, with performance-led age changes.
  2. Prepare clean references. Start with the app's documented 5-to-30-second range, using dry, close, consistent, clean speech. Keep music, room reverb, wind, and celestial effects out of the reference. Maintain several references per principal identity for neutral speech, tenderness, public projection, and fatigue.
  3. Record an audition packet for every speaking role. The cast table supplies description, emotional range, and an original audition line. Add one question, one reassurance, one urgent phrase, and one difficult place name. For historical people these are interpretive voices, not recovered authentic recordings. Use voice material and performers with permission.
  4. Verify the local generator. Test that a requested reference filename exists: the current backend can fall through to unreferenced generation if it is missing. Process one generation at a time until concurrency is tested. Handle an initial 202 model-load response and subsequent loading/busy states explicitly. Confirm the output's identity and sample rate by listening and inspecting the actual file.
  5. Generate short semantic chunks. Record a complete thought, generally one or two sentences. Capture several takes of emotional lines. Assemble longer speeches from locked phrases with deliberate room tone and pauses; do not assume a single long-generation call sustains a twelve-minute performance.
  6. Separate text from direction. Spoken text, emotion, pronunciation, movement, and mix notes are different fields. The README mentions pause tags, but support must be auditioned against the installed model; if they are read aloud, use silence in the edit instead. Do not feed the entire stage direction into TTS as speech.
  7. Finish and integrate. Keep untouched generated masters, select takes, trim clicks without clipping consonants, and normalize through a consistent dialogue pipeline. Preserve the source rate or resample once for the game's chosen audio rate. Make mono spatial speech, intelligible captions, light room sends, optional separate sacred-space effects, and loudness matched by listening. Start with broad visemes and hand-timed emotional cues before attempting full facial automation.

Use a stable filename such as JS_028_JOSEPH_MATURE_004_t03.wav. The recording manifest should retain line ID, speaker preset, age, spoken text, source/provenance, reference filename, model identifier, generation parameters, take number, duration, sample rate, approved filename, caption, pronunciation override, viseme cue file, and review status. The local backend exposes temperature, top-p, top-k and quality settings, but no seed field in its current request model; keep the generated masters because identical text is not a guarantee of reproducible audio.

Pronunciation review should cover Nauvoo, Moroni, Cowdery, Hyrum, Kirtland, Susquehanna, Follett, Whitney, Parley, Orson, Jehovah, and Adam-ondi-Ahman. Save actual auditioned pronunciations rather than forcing one guessed phonetic spelling into every model. John Taylor's hymn requires a separate sung audition and a reviewed period text/tune; speech cloning should not be assumed to provide controlled singing.

The line estimates in the cast table are planning allowances for a full campaign, not counts of finished dialogue. The exported CSV contains the current audition and scene-treatment lines only. Before recording lock, expand every interaction and selected historical passage into the same manifest, then review theology, source attribution, timing, and Joseph/Emma continuity together.

What xChurch contributes

The xChurch homepage imagines future time travel, divine eternity beyond ordinary spacetime, Christ as an Omega destination, information carried through religious metaphor, and AI-assisted interventions intended to reduce suffering. Its stated temporal ethics emphasize limited disruption, responsibility, healing, and reconciliation. It also raises the danger of butterfly effects. These are speculative beliefs and story material, not evidence that prophets were visited by engineers from the future.

The user's “breakaway Zion civilization” is a productive creative synthesis. The retrieved homepage does not itself establish a detailed civilization, a historical secret organization, or an official relationship with the LDS Church. Its language about health, intelligence, and prolific families need not become a rule deciding who deserves transformation. Give future Zion a broad human membership, including disabled people and people who never had children. Their dignity and agency fit the compassionate mission more convincingly than a biological elite.

The Mormon Transhumanist Affirmation independently links compassionate discipleship with scientific and technological participation in transformation, resurrection, renewal, and creative worlds. Treat it as that movement's interpretation. The user's Teilhard de Chardin comparison supplies the thematic image of creation converging in Christ; the game need not claim that a technological singularity is identical to LDS exaltation or that either idea proves a time machine.

The cosmology for this fiction

Use a future community of free persons whose knowledge has grown alongside their ability to love. Their technologies are instruments of service. Christ remains a personal divine presence, not a twist revealing that God was only an AI or that Joseph's faith was a fabricated software exploit. Divine agency and future human ministry can coexist in the fiction without reducing the First Vision to a machine-generated deception.

The mission discovers endangered people and relationships across eras, repairs the conditions that allow them to flourish, and learns that perfecting history by controlling its people would destroy Zion's purpose. The “secret combination” antagonist is an invented cross-era custodial network that hoards records and engineers obedience because it fears uncertainty. Its members can be challenged and reconciled. Do not assign its fictional conspiracy to actual churches, Freemasonry, ethnic communities, or living political opponents.

A playable first slice

Build one 250-to-400-meter river-bend map in three states first: 1840s, ruined future, restored Zion. Keep the seven-era design intact, but prove the expensive mechanics with three variants. Then add 1955, 1994, and 2026 as distinct causal layers, and Eden as the symbolic origin. The overall mission can braid three recurring stories: a family's memory, a watershed's health, and a community's struggle between shared knowledge and imposed control. Recurring objects change without becoming implausibly indestructible: a promise is recopied, a spring is rechanneled, a tree has descendants, a building is rebuilt. The final scene echoes the original young Joseph offering trust and asking for guidance.

Rules that keep time travel understandable

  • The historical campaign is locked. The expansion does not rescue Joseph from Carthage, insert twenty-first-century props into 1820, or reveal that the earlier revelations were false.
  • Future branches are possibilities. Helping the present makes one future more accessible; it does not prove that real history obeys a particular deterministic prophecy.
  • The portal transfers Joseph's expansion body and consenting companions. It never takes over an unwilling historical person's body or substitutes a holographic fake for a family member.
  • Carry only a small set of mission items approved for temporal transfer. A supplied tool must fit the mission's limits; the player cannot solve every age by dumping future weaponry into it.
  • Repairs act through records, relationships, shelter, water, and informed choices. They do not manufacture religious persecution because suffering is supposedly a convenient place to inject ideas.
  • Individual people remain themselves at the Omega-like culmination. Union means communion and cooperation, not compulsory absorption into a collective mind.

At the end, the player can inspect all seven states from a single stable threshold. True bubble portals

A production portal needs a destination camera derived from the source camera and entry/exit transforms, a clipped destination view, depth-correct masking, and a physical transfer rule. A planar opening can be framed by an animated spherical membrane; a genuinely curved refractive volume is a later effect with additional complexity. Initially render one destination at reduced resolution, with recursion disabled. A translucent sphere that shows an ordinary background does not satisfy the requirement to see another era with parallax.

Both era maps share a named local coordinate system and landmark anchors. Keep their mutable state separate. Preload the destination before allowing a crossing; validate the capsule's landing point, rotate velocity and view into the destination frame, move companion and audio state once, and use a crossing latch to prevent bounce-back. Dispose of off-era resources after a safe return path exists. The present and two possible futures are separate state variants, not silently overwritten copies of one map.
